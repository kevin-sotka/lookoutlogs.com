Hi. Thanks for, um… thanks for finding this.

My name is Drew Silva. This is a research project, and right now it’s mostly a cardboard box.

My dad worked fire lookouts in the Cascades, back in the late sixties and early seventies. A while ago he handed me his old logbooks. He didn’t really say why. Most of what’s in them is what you’d expect. Weather. Smoke reports. Who hiked up, and when.

But there’s a mark. A small one, the same one, showing up summer after summer. And next to it, he wrote about lights. Lights moving along the range, one mountain at a time.

So I’m going to follow them. North to south, from Mount Baker down to Crater Lake. I’ll keep my notes here as I go, and the recordings, too.

If you worked a lookout… or someone in your family did… or if you’ve ever seen something from up high that you couldn’t explain, I’d really like to hear from you. There’s a way to leave me a message on this page.

Okay. That’s the project. Thanks for listening.

A note before you start

I should say up front that I’m not a historian. I’m a guy with a box of my dad’s old logbooks and more questions than I expected to have.

My father, Abe Silva, worked fire lookouts in the Cascades from 1969 to 1974, a few years before I was born. He spent those summers alone in glass cabs on top of mountains, watching for smoke, writing down the weather, and taking a bearing whenever something burned.

Most of what he wrote is the job. But there’s a small mark that keeps showing up, usually at night, and usually near a few words about lights. I’d like to understand what he was looking at. If I’m honest, I’d also like to understand him a little better.

So this is where I’ll keep my notes, one mountain at a time, north to south. Each stop gets a log entry with what I found, the logbook pages that matter, and sources for the history, because the history here is real and I want to get it right.

Drew

The mark

A little circle with a line standing up out of it, like a pin, and then a number and a time.

It isn’t in any lookout manual I’ve found. If you’ve seen it before, in a logbook, a notebook, or the margin of a map, I’d really like to know.
