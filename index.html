The Lookout Logs

My dad worked fire lookouts in the Cascades from 1969 to 1974, and a while ago he gave me his logbooks. There’s a small mark in them that I can’t figure out. It’s usually on a night entry, and it’s usually next to a note about lights he saw from the tower.

Transcript

Hi. Thanks for, um… thanks for finding this.

My name is Drew Silva. This is a research project, and right now it’s mostly a cardboard box.

My dad worked fire lookouts in the Cascades, back in the late sixties and early seventies. A while ago he handed me his old logbooks. He didn’t really say why. Most of what’s in them is what you’d expect. Weather. Smoke reports. Who hiked up, and when.

But there’s a mark. A small one, the same one, showing up summer after summer. And next to it, he wrote about lights. Lights moving along the range, one mountain at a time.

So I’m going to follow them. North to south, from Mount Baker down to Crater Lake. I’ll keep my notes here as I go, and the recordings, too.

If you worked a lookout… or someone in your family did… or if you’ve ever seen something from up high that you couldn’t explain, I’d really like to hear from you. There’s a way to leave me a message on this page.

Okay. That’s the project. Thanks for listening.

A note before you start

I’m not a historian, and I want to say that first. Most of what I know about fire lookouts I’ve learned since my dad gave me the box, from library books and from the logbooks themselves.

My father, Abe Silva, spent six summers alone in glass cabs on top of mountains, watching for smoke, writing down the weather, and taking a bearing whenever something burned. That was a few years before I was born, so I only know that part of his life from what he wrote down.

Most of the logbooks are the job. The mark is the part that isn’t, and I’d like to know what he was looking at when he made it. I’d also like to know him a bit better, which is probably the real reason I started.

I’m going to visit each mountain where he saw the lights, starting at Baker, and post an entry here after each one. The history in these entries is real, so I’m listing my sources, and if I get something wrong I’d appreciate being told.

Drew

The mark

He drew it as a small circle with a short line coming up out of the top, a bit like a pushpin, and he always followed it with a number and a time.

I’ve looked through every lookout manual I could find and haven’t seen it in any of them. If it’s familiar to you from a logbook, a field notebook, or anywhere else, please tell me. There’s more about it in my first post.

The route

Ten stops, from Mount Baker south to Crater Lake, in the order the lights moved through his logbooks. Each one gets filled in on the map once I’ve posted the entry for it.
